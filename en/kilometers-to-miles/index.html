How to convert kilometers to miles in your head

Multiplying by 0.6 leaves you within 3% of the real value, which is plenty for most conversations. But there is a better trick, and it is a lovely mathematical coincidence: because the ratio between the mile and the kilometre (1.609) is so close to the golden ratio (1.618), the Fibonacci sequence converts kilometres to miles almost perfectly. Each number in the sequence, in kilometres, is roughly the previous one in miles:

FibonacciReal conversionError
5 km ≈ 3 miles3.10693.4%
8 km ≈ 5 miles4.9710.6%
13 km ≈ 8 miles8.07781.0%
21 km ≈ 13 miles13.04880.4%
34 km ≈ 21 miles21.12660.6%

Speeds: mph to km/h

The conversion is the same one (× 1.609344), and it is the one you need when the rental car abroad shows kilometres per hour:

Frequently asked questions about kilometers and miles

How many kilometres are in a mile?
A statute mile is exactly 1.609344 kilometres, that is, 1,609.344 metres. The figure is exact by definition since the International Yard and Pound Agreement of 1959.
How many miles are in a kilometre?
A kilometre is 0.6214 miles, a little under two thirds of a mile. To do it in your head, multiply the kilometres by 0.6: 100 km is about 62 miles.
How do you convert kilometres to miles in your head?
Multiply by 0.6 and you land within 3% of the real value. The neater trick is the Fibonacci sequence: 5 km is 3 miles, 8 km is 5 miles, 13 km is 8 miles and 21 km is 13 miles, all within 1% of the truth.
How fast is 60 mph in km/h?
60 miles per hour is 96.6 km/h. The other way round, 100 km/h is 62.1 mph and 120 km/h is 74.6 mph.
Is a nautical mile the same as a mile?
No. A statute mile is 1,609.344 metres and a nautical mile is exactly 1,852 metres: the nautical one is 15% longer. It is one minute of arc of a meridian and it is used at sea and in the air.
How many metres are in a foot and in a yard?
A foot is exactly 0.3048 metres (30.48 cm) and a yard is three feet: 0.9144 metres. An inch is exactly 2.54 cm. All three have been exact by definition since 1959.
